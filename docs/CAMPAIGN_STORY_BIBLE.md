# SPECIAL OPS — Operation Iron Veil
## Canonical campaign story bible

**Document status:** Narrative canon for production  
**Game:** SPECIAL OPS / VICTUS field demonstration  
**Genre:** Original, first-person military tactical thriller  
**Structure:** Prologue, seven operations, epilogue and credits  
**Tone:** Cinematic military action, squad-led dark comedy, investigation and environmental scare-comedy  
**Spoiler note:** This document contains the complete story, including the V-01 reveal and ending.

This is the authoritative story source for the campaign. Mission briefs, dialogue, cinematics, level objectives and environmental storytelling must implement this narrative. The production roadmap links to this document. Call of Duty is a pacing and production-quality reference only; all characters, events, locations, dialogue, missions and visual compositions must remain original.

## 1. The story in one paragraph

A few days after a series of coordinated attacks along the Northline border corridor, the Karsovian Republic is preparing to blame its neighbour, the Lydrian Republic, and authorize a major military response. Halyard Team is sent to a supposedly decommissioned Karsovian relay installation called Kestrel Station to find missing military intelligence auditor Major Elias Venn and recover the evidence he says can explain the attacks. The station is guarded by Greyline, a private security force whose orders are to keep everyone out. As Halyard moves from the forest perimeter into the facility and its underground levels, the team discovers that a faction inside Karsovia's own security establishment staged the Northline incidents to manufacture a crisis, seize emergency powers and secure control of the corridor. The original records are stored deep below the facility. Halyard must recover them before the crisis becomes an open conflict. At the end, the feared VICTUS project proves to be a clandestine records-and-rehearsal operation, while the mysterious V-01 turns out to be an inert, awkwardly placed Vanguard demonstration dummy behind a chair—not a weapon, boss or intelligent machine.

## 2. Setting, factions and the actual conspiracy

### The countries and the crisis

- **The Karsovian Republic** is the country in which the campaign takes place. Halyard Team serves in its armed forces.
- **The Lydrian Republic** is Karsovia's neighbouring state. Relations are tense, but the two countries have not declared war.
- **The Northline Corridor** is a strategically important border region containing transport links, communications infrastructure and several remote security posts.
- **Kestrel Station** is an old Karsovian military communications and records installation on the Karsovian side of Northline. It was officially decommissioned. In reality, its underground levels continued to operate under a restricted government programme.

Three days before the campaign, several Northline posts and infrastructure sites were attacked. The attacks caused real losses and triggered a serious border crisis. Public statements and early intelligence briefings attribute the attacks to Lydrian forces. Both countries have raised their readiness, and Karsovia's National Security Council is due to consider emergency border powers at dawn.

### The people behind it

A small faction within Karsovia's **Directorate of Strategic Continuity** has been manipulating the crisis. Its director, **Ilyan Kade**, believes the elected government is too cautious to retain control of Northline. He intends to use a manufactured external threat to secure emergency powers, place the corridor under permanent directorate control and extend a lucrative security arrangement with Greyline.

Kade did not create the entire regional dispute, and he does not control every institution in Karsovia. He exploited an existing period of tension and arranged for the Northline attacks to be falsely attributed to Lydria. The purpose was to make escalation appear unavoidable and to make his proposed emergency measures look necessary.

The campaign does not need to explain the mechanics of how false evidence is produced. It needs to show the contradiction between the public account and the original records, then reveal who authorized the deception and why.

### Greyline

**Greyline** is a private security contractor hired to protect Kestrel Station and the surrounding infrastructure. Its field personnel have been told that the installation contains sensitive national-security material and that unauthorized armed personnel may attempt to seize it.

Most ordinary Greyline contractors do not know the full conspiracy. Some believe they are protecting a legitimate government site. Others suspect that their orders are unlawful but remain because of loyalty, money or fear of consequences. Their presence creates the campaign's opposition, but they are not all written as identical villains.

Greyline's field commander is **Captain Dorian Sorn**. He knows the site is being used for a restricted operation, but he has not been told the whole truth. He considers Halyard an unauthorized threat to his contract and mission. Over the campaign he becomes increasingly aware that Kade intends to abandon Greyline and blame the entire operation on its contractors if the truth emerges.

### What VICTUS actually is

**VICTUS** is the codename for the restricted records, review and rehearsal programme housed beneath Kestrel Station. It was used by Kade's faction to assemble, review and maintain the approved account of the Northline incidents while keeping the original source material separate.

VICTUS is not:
- a superweapon;
- an autonomous combat system;
- a sentient artificial intelligence;
- a secret army of robots;
- a final boss.

The real evidence is mundane but consequential: the original incident records, the audit trail, the directorate's signed authorization and the Greyline contract. Together they establish that the public account was deliberately manufactured.

The final chamber contains the **Vanguard V-01**, an inert demonstration robot/dummy once used as a visual stand-in during room and camera-reference work. It was left sitting awkwardly behind a chair. Its designation appears in old room records, which makes the team expect something far more extraordinary. The reveal is deliberately anticlimactic and funny. The V-01 never moves, speaks, attacks, thinks or becomes a threat.

## 3. Why Halyard is there — and why the mission continues underground

### The initial authorization

Major Elias Venn is a Karsovian military intelligence auditor. Before he disappeared, he sent a short, encrypted distress message from the Kestrel area. He said that the Northline incident record was false, that he had found the original evidence, and that he could not safely deliver it through normal channels.

Halyard Team is authorized by Joint Operations Command to:
1. locate and recover Venn;
2. determine whether Kestrel Station is active;
3. recover the original records relevant to the Northline attacks;
4. secure the installation without causing a wider border incident.

The order is legitimate. The team is not sent to invade Lydria, punish a population or kill everyone at the site.

### Why a small team?

The installation is inside Karsovian territory, but the border crisis is politically fragile. A large military deployment could be interpreted as the beginning of a wider operation, expose the recovery mission and give the conspirators time to destroy or move the evidence. A compact Halyard deployment is authorized to verify the situation and recover Venn before the National Security Council meets.

This is a narrative reason for a focused campaign, not a claim that a small team can replace an entire army. The mission remains connected to command through the radio handler Lamplight, although the reliability of the wider command channel becomes uncertain after the team discovers evidence of an internal conspiracy.

### Why the forest, facility and perimeter?

Kestrel Station is remote. The forest is the natural approach from the available staging area. The perimeter is the only route into the station's controlled grounds. The facility contains the administrative and security records that reveal where Venn was taken. Each area is a step in the assigned recovery and evidence mission, not an arbitrary level chosen because it looks interesting.

### Why enter the bunker?

The facility's surface systems contain copies and summaries. Venn's message warns that those copies cannot prove the truth because the official record has been altered. The original source material and its audit trail are held in a separate archive in the bunker and lower basement.

When Halyard finds Venn, he confirms that the **Source Vault** is the only place where the original material, the authorization trail and the Greyline contract can be compared together. Without those originals, Kade can dismiss a copied file or a witness statement as fabricated. Venn is the witness; the Source Vault is the independent proof.

The team therefore has a clear, continuing objective: rescue Venn, recover the originals and deliver verifiable evidence before the dawn emergency session. Going deeper is the only way to finish the mission.

### Why does the team fight Greyline?

Greyline has orders to keep the station sealed. Its personnel challenge, detain or attack Halyard as the team tries to reach Venn and the records. Halyard responds to immediate threats and continues to protect the mission, but it is not sent on a mass-elimination operation.

**Combat canon:** the existing PC-7 Lantern and SP-2 Wren use fictional non-lethal pulse ammunition. Affected contractors are stunned, surrender, retreat or are taken out of the immediate encounter. The story must not describe the campaign as Halyard killing an army. Do not add graphic injury, gore or a body-count motive. Each encounter must serve a concrete story purpose: a checkpoint is held, an access route is contested, a record room is defended or the archive is being seized.

## 4. Main cast and character arcs

### Rook — the player character

Rook is the newest member of Halyard Team. The player experiences the campaign through Rook's first-person perspective. Rook is capable and already trained; the character is new to this team, not an inexperienced recruit.

Rook's arc is about earning the trust of a close unit and learning to make sound decisions when the original mission briefing stops matching reality. Do not force a detailed personality onto the player. Use concise voiced lines where required, but let the player's actions carry most of the role.

### Anchor — team lead

Anchor is disciplined, measured and accountable for the team, with exhausted deadpan humour. He initially gives Rook clear instructions and keeps the squad focused on the recovery objective. He tries to maintain professionalism when Tally gets spooked, but occasionally admits that a sound or a place is unsettling. As the mission becomes more complicated, he stops treating the briefing as the whole truth and trusts Rook to help make decisions. His arc is from controlled caution to earned mutual trust.

### Patch — medic

Patch is observant, empathetic and practical, with sharp dry humour and honest reactions when startled. She can jump at a sudden noise, then immediately insist she was checking the team's spacing. Her jokes often puncture Tally's bravado. She reminds the team that Greyline contractors are people, not faceless targets, and treats anyone who can safely be helped. Her arc is about keeping the human cost visible while the mission becomes politically larger.

### Echo — scout

Echo is quiet, analytical and attentive to details, with an understated delivery that makes her unintentionally funny. She is rarely the first to react to a noise; she is usually the first to identify its mundane source. Echo is the first member of the squad to notice that the site's records and the official timeline do not agree. Her observations move the story forward; she should not become a magical source of answers. Her arc is from detached observer to someone who openly trusts the squad with what she has found.

### Tally — communications

Tally is sociable, confident, irreverent and the squad's main chatterbox. Tally keeps the team connected to Lamplight, notices contradictory messages and helps preserve the evidence channel when the normal command picture becomes unreliable. Tally is quick with a joke and occasional strong profanity, but becomes the easiest member to startle when a howl, growl or unexplained metallic noise breaks the silence. Tally then insists they were never scared. The humour should be character-based, varied and never allowed to bury the mission.

### Lamplight — headquarters radio handler

Lamplight delivers the briefing, checks mission status and maintains the team's link to Joint Operations Command. Lamplight is not the mastermind. When conflicting instructions arrive, Lamplight reports what can and cannot be verified rather than pretending to know everything. Lamplight is usually professional, but can occasionally answer Tally's radio complaints with one perfectly timed deadpan remark. Lamplight helps route the recovered evidence to an independent review authority at the end.

### Ranger Hollis — local guide

Hollis is a civilian park ranger familiar with the woodland around Kestrel. Hollis guides Halyard along the old service route in Operation 01 and explains that the supposedly closed installation has had recent vehicle activity. Hollis knows the local wildlife calls and is quietly amused when trained operators mistake distant ridge-hound howls for something much worse. Hollis is not a secret commando or a combat sidekick. After guiding the team to the forest boundary, Hollis withdraws to a safe position and later helps identify the agreed extraction point.

### Major Elias Venn — missing intelligence auditor

Venn discovered that the Northline incident record did not match the original material. He tried to preserve the source evidence and was detained at Kestrel. He is tired and shaken but coherent, and his testimony explains what the team is looking for. He does not magically know every detail: the records in the Source Vault are needed to prove his account.

Venn is recovered in Operation 05. He remains in a secure bunker communications room while Halyard continues to the Source Vault. This avoids turning the remainder of the campaign into an escort mission and gives Venn a credible way to help over radio.

### Captain Dorian Sorn — Greyline field commander

Sorn is professional, controlled and convinced that Halyard is violating a legitimate security contract. He is the recurring face of Greyline's resistance. He does not reveal the whole conspiracy early. As Halyard uncovers the truth, Sorn realizes that Kade has withheld the purpose of VICTUS and intends to make Greyline the scapegoat. In the final confrontation, Sorn can be subdued and detained; the story does not require his death.

### Ilyan Kade — antagonist

Kade is the director of the Strategic Continuity Directorate and the architect of the Northline deception. He is calm, persuasive and convinced that the ends justify his actions. He believes emergency authority will secure the corridor and make his faction indispensable. He appears through recorded briefings, signed instructions and communications, not as a surprise action-game boss. His own records expose him.

## 5. Complete campaign, from the first scene to the last

### PROLOGUE — Orders Before Dawn

**Opening image:** A regional map in a secure briefing room. Three Northline locations are marked. News audio reports the attacks, the competing accusations and the rising tension between Karsovia and Lydria. No graphic battle montage is required.

Lamplight briefs Halyard. Major Elias Venn has disappeared after sending a distress message from Kestrel Station. The official record says the site is decommissioned, but Venn's message and recent transport activity suggest otherwise. The National Security Council will consider emergency measures at dawn. Halyard must find Venn and recover the original records before that meeting.

The camera introduces Rook, Anchor, Patch, Echo and Tally as a working team. The scene moves to the HT-4 Mule. The squad checks the cargo area and discusses the available equipment. This is the first opportunity for natural squad banter and for the player to understand the equipment interaction. Tally complains that the trunk has been packed by someone who clearly hates backs; Patch tells Tally to stop narrating the inventory; Echo points out that Tally packed it. Anchor lets the exchange run for a moment, then asks everyone to focus.

Anchor gives Rook the mission's central rule: recover the person and the proof; do not turn a tense border situation into a larger one. The Mule departs.

**Title card:** SPECIAL OPS — OPERATION IRON VEIL.

### OPERATION 01 — Green Silence
**Location:** Forest approach  
**Primary purpose:** Introduce the team in the field, establish that Kestrel is active and reach the outer perimeter.

The Mule can no longer continue along the old forestry route, so Halyard dismounts. Ranger Hollis meets the team and guides them through the woodland. Hollis explains that the station was supposed to be closed, but maintenance vehicles have been seen moving through the area at unusual hours.

The forest begins quietly. The squad talks naturally while moving. The player can inspect a small number of environmental clues with the field utility device: recent vehicle traces, a fresh service marker and discarded station packaging. These clues establish recent activity without turning the forest into a corridor of exposition.

Then a long, distant howl travels across the valley. A second answers from another direction. The squad pauses. A nearer low growl rolls through the brush, followed by leaves shaking and a branch snapping. Tally blurts, “What the fuck was that?” Anchor raises a hand to hold position. Echo listens, then notices the sound moving away from the trail. Hollis explains that ridge-hound-like wildlife calls across the valley at dusk; it is not approaching the team. Tally insists the explanation does not make the noise less rude. The squad resumes, a little more alert and a little less willing to admit it was startled. Keep the wildlife off-screen or distant: it is ambience, not an attack encounter.

Near the end of the route, Greyline personnel challenge the team. The first encounter is short and readable. Halyard identifies itself and attempts to avoid escalation; Greyline refuses to let the team pass. The player and companions defend themselves with the existing fictional non-lethal weapons. Some contractors are stunned and others withdraw or surrender.

A surrendered contractor says they were told the site was under a restricted security order and that unauthorized armed people were trying to seize it. The contractor does not know why the site was supposedly decommissioned. This is the first hint that Greyline itself may be operating on incomplete information.

Hollis takes the team to a concealed service approach and then withdraws to safety. Halyard sees the perimeter lights ahead. The station is unmistakably active.

**Operation ending:** Anchor confirms that the mission has changed from locating a signal to entering an active restricted installation. The team continues.

### OPERATION 02 — Dead Ground
**Location:** Outer perimeter and access structures  
**Primary purpose:** Prove that the site is active, establish Greyline's official cover and find Venn's trail.

Halyard reaches the perimeter checkpoint. It is not a deserted ruin: power, security lighting and routine maintenance are active. The team finds current access records and a security roster showing that Greyline has been contracted to protect Kestrel under a Strategic Continuity Directorate authorization.

Echo notices that the date on one maintenance record conflicts with the site's public decommissioning date. The detail is small but concrete: the installation has been operating under a hidden contract.

A loose warning sign taps against a fence post in the gusts. The irregular rhythm sounds briefly like footsteps. Tally turns toward it; Patch does too, then pretends she was checking the fence line. Echo identifies the sign. Anchor tells the squad that the next person to challenge an inanimate object can explain it to Lamplight in the mission report.

The team also finds evidence that Venn reached the perimeter and was transported farther inside. A damaged field recorder contains a partial message from him: the surface records cannot be trusted. Before the team can recover more, the checkpoint enters a lockdown state and Greyline moves to stop them.

The encounter is about gaining access to the service yard, not clearing a limitless wave of enemies. The squad secures the route and reaches the facility approach.

**Operation ending:** Halyard has proof that the site is active and that Venn was taken inside. The question is no longer whether the facility exists; it is what it is protecting.

### OPERATION 03 — Hard Entry
**Location:** Kestrel Station exterior, service yard and facility entrance  
**Primary purpose:** Establish Sorn as the opposing commander and get into the facility.

Halyard reaches the main facility. The exterior is a working industrial-security site, not a generic ruined base. Greyline personnel occupy the service yard and controlled entrances.

Captain Dorian Sorn first appears over the station's public-address system. He identifies Halyard and orders the team to surrender its equipment and leave. Anchor replies that the team is executing an authorized recovery and investigation order. Sorn says the installation is under a separate security mandate and refuses access. The PA speaker clips into a low metallic rasp as it shuts off. Tally mutters, “Even the intercom sounds hostile.” Echo replies that the speaker needs maintenance, not a negotiation.

The disagreement is no longer a misunderstanding that can be resolved with a routine checkpoint conversation. Sorn has orders to keep the site sealed. Halyard must defend its route into the administrative building. Use a contained encounter with clear start and end states, readable cover and opportunities for contractors to surrender.

Once inside the outer facility, the squad recovers a site manifest and a record of Venn being moved to the underground bunker. The manifest confirms that the facility's public closure was a cover for continued restricted operations.

**Operation ending:** The team enters the facility interior and hears Sorn order a full internal lockdown.

### OPERATION 04 — The Paper War
**Location:** Facility interior, operations rooms and records offices  
**Primary purpose:** Reveal the contradiction behind the Northline attacks and make the descent into the bunker necessary.

Halyard moves through the interior offices and operations spaces. The environment tells a story: active workstations beside supposedly archived equipment, recent staff belongings and rooms that were never truly abandoned. The squad finds official incident summaries, surveillance copies and a set of records that do not agree with one another.

Echo identifies the central contradiction: some of the records used to support the accusation against Lydria were created or approved after the events they claim to document. Other original references point back to Kestrel. This is not yet enough to prove who ordered the deception, but it proves that the public account is unreliable.

The team finds a recording left by Venn:

> “If you're hearing this, they moved me below. The copies are already clean. Don't take the copies. The originals are in the Source Vault. That's the only record they can't explain away.”

The recording gives the team a precise objective. They must find Venn and reach the original source archive.

A ventilation fan starts somewhere above the records office. Its uneven housing produces a low, throaty growl that seems to move along the ceiling. Tally stops mid-sentence. Echo checks the room's service diagram and identifies the fan. Patch says she would still prefer a building that doesn't sound like it's clearing its throat. The sound is unsettling for a moment, then becomes a small running joke when the fan starts again.

Sorn speaks again, now less interested in negotiation. He says the facility contains restricted national-security material and that Halyard's presence is an unauthorized breach. At the same time, Lamplight reports a conflicting recall instruction. Lamplight cannot confirm that the new order came through the same authority that approved the mission. Tally warns that the team must not treat every incoming message as trustworthy.

A facility lift or service access route leads down to the bunker.

**Operation ending:** Anchor states the new objective plainly: find Venn, recover the originals and establish who is behind the altered record.

### OPERATION 05 — Below the Line
**Location:** Main bunker, command and utility areas, holding wing  
**Primary purpose:** Rescue Venn and explain the conspiracy without removing the need to reach the Source Vault.

The bunker is a distinct environment: heavier construction, older systems, command rooms and controlled utility spaces. It should feel like a functioning military installation beneath the surface, not another copy of the facility corridors.

The heavy pressure doors groan as their seals release. Pipes knock somewhere behind the walls, and a distant metal impact echoes through the bunker. The team briefly stops to listen. Tally whispers, “If this is the basement, I don't want to meet the basement's basement.” Anchor tells Tally to keep moving. Patch admits the door noise startled her too, then immediately tells everyone not to make a thing of it.

Halyard follows Venn's movement record to a restricted holding area. Greyline personnel attempt to prevent access. The team gets through the encounter and finds Venn alive.

Patch checks Venn and helps him recover enough to speak. Venn confirms the truth: a faction inside the Strategic Continuity Directorate staged the Northline incidents and arranged for them to be blamed on Lydria. Kade wanted an emergency vote that would give his directorate control of the corridor and extend Greyline's contract. Venn discovered the discrepancy while auditing the incident record and tried to preserve the originals before he was detained.

Venn also explains why the squad cannot simply take the surface files and leave. The surface systems contain copies and approved summaries. The Source Vault below holds the original source material, the signed authorization trail and the contract that connects Kade to the operation. Without that chain, the evidence can be dismissed as a compromised copy and Venn can be portrayed as a disgruntled official.

Venn is not fit to travel through the remaining levels. Halyard secures him in a bunker communications room, where he can speak with Lamplight and support the team's return. Patch stays long enough to ensure he is stable; Anchor then leads the team onward.

The dawn emergency session is approaching. The team has no reason to continue exploring for its own sake: the originals are the evidence needed to stop the false account from becoming official policy.

**Operation ending:** Halyard descends to the lower basement and the Source Vault.

### OPERATION 06 — The Source Vault
**Location:** Substantial lower basement and archive complex  
**Primary purpose:** Recover the original evidence and expose the order behind the operation.

The lower basement is a major playable chapter with its own spatial identity. It is older and more industrial than the bunker above, with a large archive hall, service galleries, records storage and a central review area. It must feel like a separate location with scale and landmarks, not a long basement corridor.

Cooling fans produce a layered hum that resembles slow breathing in the largest archive hall. A hanging inventory tag taps against a metal rack at long intervals. The sound appears to follow the squad until Echo spots the tag moving in the airflow. Tally says, “I nearly started a fight with office stationery.” A distant archive speaker plays a clipped fragment—“...please remain...” —before cutting out. Patch looks at Tally and says, “Don't.” Tally replies, “I wasn't going to say anything.” They absolutely were.

The team reaches the Source Vault. Its contents make the conspiracy provable:
- original Northline incident source records;
- the audit trail showing how the approved account replaced the originals;
- Kade's signed authorization for the restricted operation;
- the Greyline contract and its continuation order;
- internal correspondence showing that Greyline personnel were not told the full purpose of their assignment.

The evidence shows that Kade's faction deliberately manufactured the appearance of an external attack to force emergency authority over Northline. It also shows that Greyline was intended to become the public scapegoat if the operation was exposed.

Sorn catches up with Halyard and demands that the archive be surrendered. He insists he is protecting a national-security installation. Tally points out that the same records show his contractors were to be abandoned and blamed. Sorn refuses to accept the claim immediately; he has spent the campaign believing Halyard is the threat.

A final contained encounter follows as Greyline tries to retake the archive. Halyard protects the evidence and prevents its removal. Contractors can surrender or withdraw. Sorn escapes the immediate encounter and retreats toward the central VICTUS chamber.

Halyard now has the primary proof but needs the final authorization record held in the central review room to complete the chain. The team follows Sorn into the last chamber.

**Operation ending:** The squad approaches the VICTUS chamber. The soundscape becomes quiet and controlled; the game builds expectation without turning the scene into horror.

### OPERATION 07 — V-01
**Location:** VICTUS chamber, final review room and chair area  
**Primary purpose:** Deliver the evidence, the human confrontation and the deliberately mundane V-01 reveal.

The approach to the final chamber is staged as the campaign's largest reveal. The team has heard repeated references to VICTUS and V-01. The architecture, lighting and sound suggest that something extraordinary is being kept there. The squad expects a central machine, prototype or major weapon system. On the final corridor, the machinery hum gradually fades. A heavy door releases with a low groan, then the sound drops almost completely away. The squad enters more cautiously than they want to admit.

The door opens onto a plain, carefully maintained review room. There are workstations, an old chair and a harmless Vanguard V-01 demonstration dummy sitting awkwardly behind the chair. It is visibly inert. Its label identifies it as a visual stand-in used for room and camera-reference work.

Let the silence last for a beat.

Patch: “That's V-01?”  
Tally: “I was expecting something considerably less… plastic.”  
Echo: “It's a display dummy.”  
Anchor: “The dummy isn't the mission. Find the final record.”  
Tally: “We came all this way for a mannequin?”  
Patch: “A very expensive mannequin, apparently.”  
Echo: “It's been sitting there longer than your last three jokes.”  
Tally: “And it's still the best-looking person in this room.”  
Anchor: “Put that in the report and I'll make you rewrite the whole thing.”

The humour is the payoff to the squad's accumulated reactions to howls, growls and mechanical noises. The room itself is ordinary. Do not add a scare, movement, voice, artificial intelligence, boss fight or supernatural implication to the dummy. It remains completely inert: no blink, head turn, animation, sound cue or interaction. The dummy is not the secret behind the attacks. It is simply the most anticlimactic object in a very heavily protected room.

The team accesses the final review record. Kade's own signed instructions and recorded statement connect him to the Northline deception. The record confirms his motive: emergency powers, control of the corridor and a long-term security contract. It also confirms that he planned to blame Greyline's field personnel if the scheme was exposed.

Sorn arrives and confronts Halyard. He has now seen enough of the archive to understand that Kade used his unit. He demands the final record, partly out of fear that the evidence will destroy his people. Anchor tells him that surrendering the evidence will not protect Greyline; it will only protect Kade. Sorn makes one final attempt to seize the storage core. Halyard stops him in a short final encounter. Sorn is stunned and detained, not killed.

Tally and Lamplight establish a confirmed channel to an independent Karsovian oversight authority. Halyard transmits the evidence package while retaining the original archive for verification. Venn confirms by radio that the recovered records match the source material he found. The mission's central question is answered: the Northline attacks were arranged by Kade's faction and falsely attributed to Lydria.

**Operation ending:** The evidence is secured and transmitted. The team heads back toward the surface.

### EPILOGUE — First Light

The squad emerges from Kestrel as dawn begins. Hollis is waiting at the agreed safe extraction point. Venn is brought out separately after Lamplight coordinates the recovery. The team regroups beside the Mule before extraction.

Lamplight reports that the independent oversight authority has accepted the evidence. The National Security Council's emergency vote is suspended pending an investigation. Karsovia and Lydria have agreed to pause further escalation while the Northline record is examined. Kade's position is under investigation; Greyline's contract is frozen. Do not pretend that one mission instantly fixes every political problem. The team has prevented the immediate false account from becoming the basis for a wider conflict.

Anchor acknowledges Rook's contribution. Patch checks that everyone is ready to leave. Echo quietly confirms that the recovered record is complete. Tally tries to add the V-01 dummy to the official mission report; Anchor tells Tally to leave it out. Tally says the report needs “one accurate photograph of Gary, the only member of VICTUS who stayed in his chair.” Anchor tells Tally to delete the nickname. Echo quietly says the nickname is already in the evidence metadata because Tally typed it into a field label. The squad shares one last laugh before the Mule departs.

The final image is the team departing together as the sun comes up over the forest. The campaign ends with the immediate operation resolved, the conspiracy exposed and the squad intact. Credits follow.

## 6. The dramatic logic, in order

The campaign must always preserve this chain of cause and effect:

1. The Northline attacks create a real crisis and an imminent political decision.
2. Venn discovers that the accepted record is false and sends a distress message.
3. Halyard receives a legitimate order to find Venn and recover the original evidence.
4. The forest leads to Kestrel; the active perimeter proves the site was never truly closed.
5. Greyline blocks access because it has been contracted to protect the station.
6. Facility records show that the public account and the original timeline do not match.
7. Venn's message identifies the Source Vault as the only place to prove the discrepancy.
8. The bunker contains Venn, who explains the conspiracy and confirms the need for the originals.
9. The lower basement contains the original source material, Kade's authorization and the Greyline contract.
10. The final chamber supplies the last record, the Sorn confrontation and the V-01 comic reveal.
11. Halyard transmits verified evidence before the emergency vote, preventing immediate escalation.
12. The team extracts together at dawn.

If a level, encounter, cinematic or prop does not support one of these steps or a meaningful character moment, reconsider its place in the campaign.

## 7. Required cinematic sequence

The existing campaign/cinematics brief requires at least twelve purposeful beats. Use this story to define their content:

1. **Opening briefing:** Northline map, attacks, Venn's disappearance, dawn deadline and Halyard's assignment.
2. **Mule arrival:** the complete squad, vehicle arrival, equipment trunk interaction and departure.
3. **Forest reveal:** Hollis meets the team; the woodland opens toward the active station.
4. **First Greyline contact:** a brief challenge, the first non-lethal encounter and a surrender.
5. **Perimeter reveal:** active security infrastructure and the first proof of a hidden contract.
6. **Sorn's warning:** his first direct address over the facility public-address system.
7. **Interior discovery:** Echo identifies the contradictory incident records; Venn's message is played.
8. **Bunker descent:** the team commits to the underground route because the originals are below.
9. **Venn recovery:** Patch checks him, Venn explains the conspiracy and gives the Source Vault objective.
10. **Source Vault reveal:** the scale of the lower basement and the original records.
11. **V-01 reveal and final confrontation:** quiet setup, inert dummy, brief squad humour, Kade's proof and Sorn's defeat/surrender.
12. **First-light epilogue:** evidence accepted, emergency vote suspended, team reunited and credits.

Cinematics may be broken into shorter in-engine moments. Do not make every beat a long cutscene. Each must transition cleanly back to first-person control.

## 8. Dialogue and performance rules

The campaign uses squad-led dark comedy and occasional environmental scare-comedy. The full voice, profanity, sample dialogue and sound direction are specified in `docs/SQUAD_BANTER_AND_AMBIENCE.md`; read it before authoring lines or ambience.

- The squad must sound like people who know one another. They can disagree, tease, swear when startled, complain about uncomfortable places and answer each other directly.
- Give each operator a distinct comic rhythm: Anchor is dry and controlled; Patch is practical and sharply observant; Echo is understated and calmly identifies mundane causes; Tally is irreverent and easily startled but pretends otherwise.
- Use occasional strong profanity when it fits a genuine reaction (“What the fuck was that?” / “Oh, for fuck's sake”), not as constant filler. Keep humour non-sexual; do not use slurs or graphic injury jokes.
- Do not force Rook to deliver constant quips. Keep player-character lines brief and situational.
- The humour should puncture tension without trivialising the Northline attacks, Venn's detention or the consequences of Kade's conspiracy.
- Hollis can explain woodland sounds with local familiarity. Lamplight remains professional with occasional dry radio responses.
- Venn is precise and tired, not a speech-making exposition machine. Sorn remains controlled and severe. Kade remains calm and self-justifying.
- A character who makes a joke should receive a visible listener reaction where practical: a look, eye-roll, small grin, exhale, head shake or gesture.
- Fear must be visible in the squad's behaviour: a hand signal to pause, a quick turn toward a sound, a tighter formation, a shared glance or a brief hesitation. Do not make trained operators scream or behave incompetently.
- Every important factual reveal must be supported by an object, record, scene or witness in addition to dialogue.
- Subtitles, speaker identity, lip-sync, facial performance, gaze and spatial audio must agree. Do not let jokes overlap mission-critical information.
- Do not use the same reaction line repeatedly. Contextual dialogue needs cooldowns, state checks and variation.
- The V-01 dummy is the final mundane punchline, not a horror prop. It must remain completely inert.

## 9. Environmental storytelling and optional discoveries

Use a small number of optional discoveries to reward attention without hiding essential plot information:
- a forestry notice showing Kestrel's public closure date;
- a current maintenance record that contradicts that date;
- a Greyline contract summary showing the restricted security arrangement;
- Venn's incomplete field note;
- an internal directorate memo that hints at emergency powers;
- a record showing Greyline's planned scapegoating.

Essential story information must never depend on finding every optional item. The main path provides the necessary facts in a clear sequence. The field utility device can identify authored points of interest, but it must not turn the story into a generic wall of highlighted objects.

Environmental sound is also storytelling. The forest has distant animal howls, answering calls and a brief low growl from wildlife beyond the trail; the perimeter has a tapping sign and fence resonance; the facility has a growling ventilation fan and faulty PA speaker; the bunker has pressure-door groans and pipe knocks; the lower basement has cooling fans, echoing tags and clipped maintenance announcements. These are plausible environmental sources, not supernatural clues or combat encounters. The detailed staged sequences, sound behaviour, sample dialogue and implementation checks live in `docs/SQUAD_BANTER_AND_AMBIENCE.md`.

A scare should have a source that can be inferred, seen or explained. Let the squad stop, listen and react before a teammate identifies the cause. The player must retain control except for a purposeful authored cinematic. Do not use random loud jumpscares, animal attacks or noises that conceal a required objective.

## 10. Tone, comedy and environmental scare canon

The game's target tone is **military dark comedy with atmospheric scare-comedy**, not straight parody and not horror. The campaign should keep its serious geopolitical stakes while allowing the squad to sound like tired, imperfect people under pressure.

- Use `docs/SQUAD_BANTER_AND_AMBIENCE.md` as the detailed tone and implementation reference.
- Environmental howls, growls, rattles, metallic groans, ventilation noise and radio artefacts should create suspense and occasional false alarms. Their sources are wildlife, wind, old infrastructure or machinery.
- The squad can be genuinely startled. Give each operator distinct body-language reactions and let the joke follow the pause.
- Include occasional strong profanity and irreverent non-sexual humour. Do not write explicit sexual jokes, slurs, graphic injury jokes or jokes aimed at real-world protected groups.
- Do not make every line a joke. The story, evidence trail and political consequences remain sincere.
- The final V-01 reveal must pay off the accumulated tension by being completely ordinary and motionless. The dummy is never alive, haunted, intelligent or threatening.

## 11. Canon and implementation constraints

These rules prevent later implementation from drifting away from the agreed story:

- The player character is Rook. Halyard's established cast is Anchor, Patch, Echo and Tally. Preserve their existing roles and voices.
- Hollis remains a civilian ranger and guide for the forest operation.
- Lamplight remains the headquarters radio handler.
- Use the existing fictional non-lethal pulse-weapon direction. Greyline encounters are not a mass-killing campaign.
- Greyline is a contracted force with mixed knowledge and motives. Do not write every contractor as a fanatic.
- Kade's faction is responsible for the deception. Do not imply that all Karsovian institutions or all citizens support it.
- Lydria is not secretly responsible for the Northline attacks.
- VICTUS is a records/rehearsal programme, not a superweapon, combat AI or sentient machine.
- V-01 is an inert Vanguard demonstration dummy, awkwardly sitting behind a chair. It is a short comedic reveal, not a horror beat or boss.
- The Source Vault is the reason Halyard enters and continues through the bunker and lower basement.
- The ending resolves the immediate Northline crisis but leaves the broader political inquiry realistic and incomplete.
- Preserve first-person gameplay. External cameras are for authored cinematics only.
- Do not copy Call of Duty missions, dialogue, characters, maps, logos, signature shots or proprietary assets.
- Do not add gore or graphic injury presentation. Keep combat fictional, non-lethal and readable.
- Do not create new systems solely to tell the story when existing VICTUS and game systems can express it. Prefer authored dialogue, state changes, existing inventory/equipment interactions, records, doors, scene transitions and current cinematic primitives.
